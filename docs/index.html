MOTIVATION AND SCOPE

Our Research

Resource wealth should accelerate development, yet most resource-rich emerging economies remain trapped in low-complexity exports.

Primary Questions

  • How successful have emerging market economies been at developing value‐added in‐dustries from natural resource extraction?
  • What factors explain the divergence across countries?

Secondary Questions

  • How sustainable are these shifts in terms of fiscal revenues, employment and export diversification?
  • How have they supported or hindered credit quality?
  • What policy levers and institutional conditions support or hinder the transition?
FROM DETERMINISM TO CONDITIONAL MECHANISMS

The Resource Curse Is Conditional

The debate has shifted from whether resources harm growth to identifying the conditions under which they do.

Mehlum, Moene & Torvik (2006)

Institutions determine outcomes

Resources impede growth only where institutions are "grabber-friendly". Where rule of law is strong, resources accelerate growth.

Venables (2016)

Consumption crowds out investment

Resource-rich governments consistently fail to convert extraction rents into human capital, infrastructure, and financial assets. Adjusted net savings are strongly negative.

Van der Ploeg & Poelhekke (2009)

Volatility is the indirect curse

Resource abundance boosts growth directly but undermines it through macroeconomic volatility, shifting investment toward short-term, low-return projects.

Isham et al. (2005)

Resource type matters

Point-source resources (oil, diamonds) concentrate rents among elites. Diffuse resources (agriculture, forestry) spread benefits more broadly.

Our approach: We test Venables' investment hypothesis using Mehlum et al.'s interaction methodology, while ML techniques explore which structural predictors emerge beyond the theory-driven specification.

QUANTITATIVE ANALYSIS USING A DUAL APPROACH

Econometrics And Machine Learning Approaches

Two approaches providing complementary perspectives to identify key predictors of industrial upgrading.

Econometrics

Econometric models based on analysis in investment theory. Testing intertemporal investment theory of Venables (2016), using identification inspired by Mehlum et al. (2006).

Machine Learning

Supervised Linear regularisation techniques (LASSO, RIDGE, Elastic Net) along with unsupervised non-linear approaches (Random Forest and PCA) to take a theory-agnostic approach.

HOW CAN WE MEASURE THE SOPHISTICATION OF AN ECONOMY?

The Economic Complexity Framework

The ECI is a measure developed by the Harvard Growth Lab to capture the degree of "know-how" accumulated by a country. It helps track the transition from low complexity activities (e.g., subsistence farming, oil extraction) towards high complexity activities (electronics, machinery, and chemicals).

How it works: Based on trade data to calculate two key measures: Diversity (number of products exported) and Ubiquity (number of countries exporting those products).
